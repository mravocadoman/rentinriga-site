How to rent a flat in Riga as a foreigner

Six steps from setting a budget to getting your keys, with the costs, the Latvian words you’ll meet in listings, and the rules that protect you. Updated for 2026.

Skip the search: get offers Find my neighbourhood

1. Set a realistic budget

Across Riga listings from March to October 2026, the average asking rent was €336 a month for a 1-room flat (studio), €489 for a 2-room flat (one bedroom) and €709 for a 3-room flat (two bedrooms), before utilities. Central areas cost more. See rent prices by neighbourhood.

Plan for more than the rent when you sign:

  • Deposit: usually one month’s rent. Latvia’s Residential Tenancy Law caps it at two months.
  • Agency fee: commonly 50% of one month’s rent on agency listings. Flats rented straight from the owner often have none.
  • Utilities: heating, water, waste, the building management fee and electricity are usually paid on top. Heating is the big one in winter, and it costs far more in an un-insulated building.

The move-in cost calculator adds it up for your rent.

2. Choose where to live

Think about your commute first, then what you want around you: cafés and walkability in the centre, parks and quiet in areas like Teika or Mežaparks, or lower rents in large estates like Purvciems and Imanta. The neighbourhood matcher scores all eleven areas on your budget, workplace and priorities, and each has its own neighbourhood guide.

4. View the flat safely

Never pay a deposit or reservation fee for a flat you haven’t seen in person or on a live video call where someone walks through it. A known scam aimed at newcomers is a fake listing with copied photos that asks for money up front. At the viewing:

  • Ask for last winter’s utility bills, not just the summer ones.
  • Check the windows, the water pressure, the heating and where the internet comes from.
  • Ask who owns the flat. A real owner or agent can show it; ownership can be checked in the Land Register (zemesgramata.lv).
  • Take photos of everything, including existing damage, before you move in.

5. Sign a proper lease

Under the Residential Tenancy Law (in force since 2021), a lease has to be in writing and for a fixed term. It should state the rent and how and when it can change, the deposit, who pays which bills, and an inventory of the furniture. Tenants can usually end a lease with one month’s written notice. Registering the lease in the Land Register is optional, but an unregistered lease can end when a new owner registers the flat, so ask about it for a long stay. The landlord has to return any unused part of the deposit when you move out, unless the contract says otherwise. Read the law itself at likumi.lv.

6. Move in and register

If you live in Latvia on a residence permit or an EU registration certificate, declare your place of residence within one month of moving in. You can do it online at latvija.gov.lv, and your lease is the legal basis for the address. EU citizens staying longer than three months also need a registration certificate from the Office of Citizenship and Migration Affairs. Check the current rules at pmlp.gov.lv.
