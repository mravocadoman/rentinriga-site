The short answer

A single person renting a studio in Riga should budget about €1,205 a month in 2026: €336 for the average 1-room flat plus about €869 for everything else, going by Numbeo’s crowd-sourced estimate. A family of four in a two-bedroom flat needs about €3,631.

For scale, the average take-home pay in Latvia was €1,401 a month in the second quarter of 2026, and half of all full-time workers took home less than €1,168 (CSP). Rent is the one cost you have real control over, so where you live matters most.

Rent: the biggest cost

The average asking rent across Riga was €336 for a 1-room flat (a studio), €489 for a 2-room flat (one bedroom) and €709 for a 3-room flat (two bedrooms) between March and October 2026. Latvian listings count every room, so a “2-room” flat has a living room and one bedroom.

Prices vary a lot by area. The cheapest studios on average were in Teika (€300), Imanta (€305), Purvciems (€310); new-build districts and the centre cost more.

Utilities, heating and internet

Utilities are almost always paid on top of rent. Most of Riga is on district heating: Rīgas siltums supplies about 76% of the heat the city needs. The building’s insulation matters as much as your own habits. Rīgas siltums’ own example for a 45 m² flat is about €116–142 a month in December and January in an un-insulated building, against €46–57 in an insulated one (excluding VAT). Ask to see last winter’s bills before you sign.

  • Electricity: €0.27 per kWh with all taxes for a typical small household in the first half of 2026 (CSP), so every 100 kWh costs about €27.
  • Internet: about €16.85 a month for 60 Mbps or faster (Numbeo).
  • Mobile: about €17.46 a month for a plan with 10 GB or more of data (Numbeo).

Numbeo’s users put basic utilities for a larger 85 m² flat at about €332 a month, averaged across the year.

Getting around

Riga’s buses, trams and trolleybuses run on one ticket system. A monthly ticket costs €30, a 90-minute ticket with transfers €1.50 and a 24-hour ticket €5. Fares have not changed since 2023, and the mayor said in March 2026 that they would not go up. Students studying in Riga pay €12 a month on a personalised e-ticket.

The centre, Old Town, Teika and Āgenskalns are walkable or a short tram ride from most offices, which can save the ticket altogether.

How to spend less

  • Choose the area before the flat. A 1-bedroom flat in Purvciems or Imanta costs about €250 a month less than in the centre.
  • Ask about insulation. Renovated buildings can halve the winter heating bill.
  • Rent from the owner or negotiate the fee. Agency fees are commonly half a month’s rent; many private landlords charge none.
  • Never pay before a viewing. Fake listings that ask for a deposit up front are the most common scam aimed at newcomers.

Quick answers

How much does it cost to live in Riga per month?

About €1,205 a month for one person renting an average studio: €336 rent plus roughly €869 of other costs (Numbeo, October 2026). A family of four in a two-bedroom flat needs about €3,631.

Is Riga cheap to live in?

Riga is cheaper than most Western European capitals, but local wages are lower too: average take-home pay in Latvia was €1,401 a month in Q2 2026. A studio costs €336 on average.

Are utilities included in rent in Riga?

Usually not. Heating, water, waste, the building management fee and electricity are paid on top. Heating costs most in winter, especially in buildings without insulation.

How much is public transport in Riga?

A monthly ticket costs €30 and a 90-minute ticket €1.50. Students pay €12 a month.
