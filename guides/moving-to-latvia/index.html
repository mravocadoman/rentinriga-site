The checklist, in order

  1. Check which route applies to you

    EU, EEA and Swiss citizens can live and work in Latvia without a permit. If you stay longer than three months, you register (step 4).

    Everyone else needs a residence permit. The main routes are employment, the EU Blue Card, study and family. For a work permit, the employer first registers the vacancy with the State Employment Agency, and it has to stay open for 10 working days before a non-EU candidate can be hired. You apply at a Latvian embassy or consulate, or at the Office of Citizenship and Migration Affairs (PMLP) if you are already in Latvia legally. Permanent residence is possible after five years. Check the current state fees on PMLP’s fee page.

  2. Line up your work, study or business

    Your permit and your budget both depend on it. Check a salary offer with our take-home pay calculator, and see what a month costs in the cost of living guide.

  3. Find a home

    Your lease is the legal basis for registering your address, so it comes early. Choose an area first: the neighbourhood matcher scores eleven Riga areas on your budget, commute and priorities. Then read how to rent a flat in Riga for deposits, agency fees and the lease, or send one request and let agencies come to you.

  4. EU citizens: get a registration certificate

    If you are an EU, EEA or Swiss citizen staying longer than three months, register in person at a PMLP office with a valid passport or ID card. See PMLP’s page for EU citizens for what to bring.

  5. Get a personal code

    Latvia’s personal identity number (personas kods) is how banks, doctors and e-services identify you. Non-EU nationals receive one with a temporary or permanent residence permit; a visa does not give one. Others can ask PMLP to add them to the Register of Natural Persons, which assigns a code. Details: pmlp.gov.lv.

  6. Declare your place of residence

    Declare your address within one month of starting to live there. It is free online at latvija.gov.lv, or €4.27 in person. Internet bank logins no longer work on latvija.gov.lv (since 1 January 2025): sign in with an eID card, eParaksts mobile, a qualified Smart-ID or another EU eID. Most of these are linked to a personal code, so get the code first.

  7. Register with a family doctor

    State-funded healthcare covers, among others, Latvian citizens and non-citizens, EU, EEA and Swiss citizens who work or are self-employed in Latvia and their families, permanent residence permit holders, and their children under 18. Temporary residence permit holders are generally not on the list and pay the clinic’s prices, so check whether you need private insurance. If you are covered, register with a family doctor (ģimenes ārsts): their referral unlocks most state-paid care. Changes to these rules were under discussion in 2025–26, so check the National Health Service for the current list.

  8. Sort out getting around

    Riga’s monthly public transport ticket costs €30, a 90-minute ticket €1.50, and students studying in Riga pay €12 a month on a personalised e-ticket.

Your first weeks in Riga

  • Budget for the start. Moving in usually costs the first month’s rent, a deposit of about one month (capped at two by law) and, through an agency, a fee of commonly half a month’s rent. The move-in cost calculator adds it up.
  • Expect a lower wage than in Western Europe and lower rents. Average take-home pay was €1,401 in Q2 2026, and an average 1-bedroom flat costs €489.
  • Prepare for winter. Heating is the biggest utility bill from November to March. A renovated, insulated building can halve it.
  • Keep your documents together. Passport, permit or registration certificate, lease and personal code are asked for again and again.

Quick answers

Do EU citizens need a visa or permit to move to Latvia?

No. EU, EEA and Swiss citizens can live and work in Latvia freely, but must register at PMLP if they stay longer than three months.

How do I get a residence permit in Latvia?

Most non-EU nationals apply through work (with an employer as sponsor), the EU Blue Card, study or family. You apply at a Latvian embassy or consulate, or at PMLP if you are already legally in Latvia.

How long do I have to declare my address in Latvia?

One month from when you start living there. Declaring online at latvija.gov.lv is free; in person it costs €4.27.

How do I get a personal code in Latvia?

Non-EU nationals receive one with a residence permit. Others can ask PMLP to add them to the Register of Natural Persons, which assigns a code.
